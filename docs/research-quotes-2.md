# Research: future-hopeful quotes (27 Oct 2026, web search only; Q2 = Berners-Lee, Weaving the Web)

# Pre-2000 hopeful quotes about the internet: sources check

Labels: **VERIFIED (index)** means an exact-phrase search returned the primary source, or a faithful reprint of it, with the sentence in it. **UNVERIFIED** means only secondary sources (quote sites, reviews, aggregators) returned it. I could not open any page directly (no WebFetch or archive), so page numbers come from secondary citations.

## 1. Nicholas Negroponte (1995): MISATTRIBUTION FLAG
- **Claimed:** "Digital technology can be a natural force drawing people into greater world harmony."
- **Finding:** I could not match this as a sentence from *Being Digital*. The Elon University "Imagining the Internet" database credits the wording to **Steve Lohr's 1995 New York Times article**. That article says Negroponte was confident technology would "be a natural force drawing people into greater world harmony." It is a journalist's paraphrase, not Negroponte's own sentence. Do not cite it as "Being Digital, p. X".
- **Verbatim alternative (Wired column, 1995, previewing the book):** "Being digital is positive. It can flatten organizations, globalize society, decentralize control, and help harmonize people in ways beyond not knowing whether you are a dog." UNVERIFIED. Elon quotes it, but wired.com was not returned.
- **Book (Epilogue, "An Age of Optimism", pp. 228–231):** the book says being digital has four qualities: "decentralizing, globalizing, harmonizing, and empowering". UNVERIFIED (only secondary sources and a Brown course note cite pp. 228–229).
- **Fit:** future-hopeful and global. The "dog" joke (from a 1993 cartoon) would confuse a 16-year-old second-language reader. Critics call Negroponte a techno-utopian.
- Sources: https://www.elon.edu/u/imagining/?p=25066 , https://elon.edu/u/imagining/?p=26464

## 2. Tim Berners-Lee (c. 1998, W3C)
- **Text:** "The dream behind the Web is of a common information space in which we communicate by sharing information."
- **Work:** "The World Wide Web: A very short personal history" (w3.org). Page metadata puts it at about 1998. The same text also appears in the W3C FAQ.
- **URL:** https://www.w3.org/People/Berners-Lee/ShortHistory.html. **VERIFIED (index).**
- **Fit:** primary source, the Web's own inventor, and no political baggage. It is not strongly about the future ("dream" helps), and "common information space" is a little abstract. It is 18 words.

## 2b. Tim Berners-Lee, *Weaving the Web* (1999)
- **Text:** "The Web is more a social creation than a technical one. I designed it for a social effect—to help people work together—and not as a technical toy."
- **Page:** Today in Science gives p. 123 (2004 printing). A Tohoku University excerpt gives p. 133. The page is disputed.
- **URLs:** https://todayinsci.com/B/BernersLee_Tim/BernersLeeTim-Quotations.htm , https://www2.sal.tohoku.ac.jp/~gothit/timweaving.html. **UNVERIFIED** (excerpts only, no publisher scan). Punctuation (dash or comma) varies between reprints.
- **Fit:** plain English, about people working together, and no baggage. It describes the inventor's intent rather than a prediction. The first two sentences (about 24 words) work well alone.

## 3. Marshall McLuhan, *The Gutenberg Galaxy* (1962)
- **Text:** "The new electronic interdependence recreates the world in the image of a global village."
- **Page:** p. 36 per the McLuhan Estate quotation dictionary. Sage encyclopedia says p. 43.
- **URL:** https://themmm.marshallmcluhan.com/?p=103695. **UNVERIFIED.** The estate dictionary is authoritative, but it is not the book's text.
- **Fit:** famous and short. The vocabulary is hard ("interdependence"). McLuhan was **not** optimistic: he warned the global village could bring retribalization and conflict. So "hopeful" misreads him, and he was talking about TV and radio, not computers.

## 4. Arthur C. Clarke, BBC *Horizon* (1964)
- **Text:** "Men will no longer commute, they will communicate." Context: "it will be possible in that age, perhaps only 50 years from now, for a man to conduct his business from Tahiti or Bali just as well as he could from London."
- **URL:** secondary only (https://fortune.com/2024/05/29/arthur-c-clarke-space-odyssey-remote-work-ai-predictions-bbc , https://www.themarginalian.org/2011/09/30/arthur-c-clarke-the-future-1964/). BBC domains were blocked. **UNVERIFIED.** Transcripts differ (comma or dash).
- **Fit:** short and memorable, but about work and travel, not voice, peace or self-government. The 1974 ABC "console in his own house" line is about home banking (UNVERIFIED, aggregators only) and fits even less.

## 5. J.C.R. Licklider & Robert W. Taylor, "The Computer as a Communication Device", *Science and Technology*, April 1968
- **Text (opening sentence):** "In a few years, men will be able to communicate more effectively through a machine than face to face."
- **URL:** https://collablab.northwestern.edu/CollabolabDistro/nucmc/LickliderAndTaylor-TheComputerAsACommunicationDevice-ScienceAndTech-1968.pdf (a reprint of the paper). **VERIFIED (index).**
- **Text 2:** "life will be happier for the on-line individual because the people with whom one interacts most strongly will be selected more by commonality of interests and goals than by accidents of proximity." **UNVERIFIED.** A domain-restricted search did not return the primary PDF; only secondary sources (Living Library, White House 2009 blog) did.
- **Fit:** clearly about the future and very early (1968). "Men" is dated and gendered. Text 2 is hopeful but hard ("commonality", "proximity"). Text 1 is about effectiveness, not humanity.

## 6. Howard Rheingold, *The Virtual Community* (1993), Introduction
- **Text:** "The technology that makes virtual communities possible has the potential to bring enormous leverage to ordinary citizens at relatively little cost—intellectual leverage, social leverage, commercial leverage, and most important, political leverage." (The tail is from memory. The search confirmed only "has the potential to bring enormous leverage to ordinary citizens at relatively little cost".)
- **URL:** secondary only (https://www.neilthewriter.co.uk/?p=800). **UNVERIFIED.**
- **Fit:** the best match on theme (ordinary citizens, political power). But it is long, uses "leverage", and the same passage warns that governments and corporations will try to control access. His final chapter, "Disinformocracy", is ambivalent: virtual communities "could help citizens revitalize democracy, or they could be luring us into an attractively packaged substitute for democratic discourse" (Elon, marked partial: UNVERIFIED).

## 7. Douglas Engelbart, "Augmenting Human Intellect: A Conceptual Framework" (SRI, 1962)
- **Text (end of a list of benefits):** "...better solutions, and the possibility of finding solutions to problems that before seemed insoluble."
- **URL:** https://www.lri.fr/~mbl/FundHCI/papers/Englebart-Augmenting62.pdf (a reprint of the report). **VERIFIED (index)** for that fragment.
- **Misattribution flag:** "our collective ability to solve complex, urgent problems on a global scale" is the **Doug Engelbart Institute's later mission wording**, not 1962. The often-shared "complexity and urgency of the problems..." line is from 2004, which is after the cutoff.
- **Fit:** hopeful, but it is a fragment of a list and abstract. It needs framing to make sense.

## 8. Carl Sagan / Kofi Annan
- **Sagan:** I found no pre-2000 hopeful line about computer networks. His famous 1995 *Demon-Haunted World* passage is a **warning**, so it does not fit. Reject.
- **Annan:** I found nothing from 1997–99. The nearest are April 2000 ("The Internet is the fastest growing instrument of communication in the history of civilization", Reuters, UNVERIFIED) and 2003 ("a powerful force that can and must be harnessed to our global mission of peace and development"). Both are after the cutoff, and Annan is a political figure. Reject.

## Top 3 (fit × verifiability)
1. **Berners-Lee, "The dream behind the Web is of a common information space in which we communicate by sharing information."** (w3.org, c. 1998). VERIFIED. It is clean, primary and non-political. The weakness is a slightly abstract phrase.
2. **Berners-Lee, *Weaving the Web* (1999): "The Web is more a social creation than a technical one. I designed it for a social effect—to help people work together."** It has the plainest English and fits "people together" best. It is UNVERIFIED, so cite the book without a page number, or check a library copy.
3. **Licklider & Taylor (1968): "In a few years, men will be able to communicate more effectively through a machine than face to face."** VERIFIED and truly future-looking. It needs a note on the gendered "men" and on its 1968 date.

Honourable mention: Rheingold has the best theme (ordinary citizens and political power), but it is long, UNVERIFIED, and hedged in context. Avoid the Negroponte "world harmony" line as a book quote; it is a 1995 NYT paraphrase.
