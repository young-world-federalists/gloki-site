# Page concept: directions for the GDI home page (26 Oct 2026)

Impeccable new-work method, step "Create or replace the visual world", run in its documented
fallback: the `concept-seed` binary could not run in this session (the permission check blocked
code from outside), so there was no dice roll and the order below is our own judgement. No image
generation either, so the build is **code-led**: directions carry a first-screen sketch, and the
ambition lives in the written direction contract. Mode: **Persuade**. Copy: `docs/copy/draft-d-merged.md`.

## What the page must carry

- **Mechanism, one sentence:** you get in when four people vouch for you and you take your place
  between them, so every member holds exactly four links, in a network its members carry and no
  one owns.
- **The audience's scene:** a link forwarded in a YWF chapter's WhatsApp group, opened on a mid-range
  Android outdoors or on a bus, on paid data; also builders and funders on laptops. Bright light,
  small screens, English often a second or third language.
- **Cultural home:** world citizenship and world federalism (world parliament, global citizens'
  assemblies, the World Passport), civic voting rituals, hobbyists who carry infrastructure (ham
  radio, FlightRadar24 antennas), open-source and mesh networks.
- **What the first screen must prove:** this is about real people counted once, it's honest about
  what works, and you can sign up right here.
- **Fixed:** brand blue `#3b82f6` (never as a ground behind body-size white text), the GDI network
  mark, AA contrast, 360px first, under 200 KB, no third-party requests, one h1.

## The rut (kept out of the list)

- **The category page:** dark mode, a glowing globe or node network, gradient text, "Web3" energy.
  This reads as crypto to the exact readers we're trying to win back.
- **Its predictable opposite:** the warm NGO page, cream ground, serif display, photos of "diverse
  youth", soft cards.
- **The brief's literal picture** (a node-and-line network) gets at most one slot (no. 7).

## Seven worlds the audience knows, by resonance

| # | World | Why it can carry the mechanism | How it looks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Isotype** (Neurath and Arntz's picture statistics, Vienna, 1920s–30s) | Built so people with any language or reading level can read data. Its rule is one figure = one count, which is exactly "each person counts once". An internationalist hope from the 1920s, mirroring the 1990s one | White ground, flat black and one or two signal colours, rows of identical pictogram people, a geometric sans |
| 2 | **The mended fishing net** | Every knot in a hand-made net joins four strands, which is the four-link rule. Fishing communities on every coast mend nets together, so the net is carried by the people who use it | Bright nylon twine blue and orange, knots, mesh drawn line by line on a light ground |
| 3 | **Polling-station kit**: indelible finger ink, ballot slips, stamps | The violet ink on a voter's finger is one person, one vote, recognised from Nairobi to Delhi | Indelible-ink violet, ballot-paper grids and tick boxes, red rubber stamps, a condensed grotesk |
| 4 | **QSL cards** (radio amateurs mail a card to confirm each contact) | A person-to-person confirmation ritual across borders, run by hobbyists who carry the radio network. It ties to Reticulum and LoRa | Bold call-sign lettering, a different card per country, date stamps, postal marks |
| 5 | **The hemicycle** (parliament seat diagrams) | Each dot is one person. A world parliament is the federalist dream | Semicircle of dots in party colours, institutional sans |
| 6 | **Passport and visa stamps** (World Passport) | World citizenship and entry | Guilloche patterns, stamp inks, machine-readable lines. *Contradicts "no ID papers"* |
| 7 | **The literal trust graph** | Shows the four-link rule directly | Nodes and edges; close to the category rut |

There are four material families: data graphics (1, 5, 7), craft (2), civic ritual (3), and
printed ephemera (4, 6).

## Directions

### A. One figure, one person (Isotype) — our lead

- **Thesis:** the page counts people the way Isotype did: one figure is one real person. It refuses
  the glowing network and the stock-photo NGO page.
- **Own world:**
  - White ground, ink-black figures, brand blue for people who have been vouched in, vermilion for
    "not yet".
  - Self-hosted geometric sans for headings (Jost, OFL), body in Atkinson Hyperlegible (OFL, drawn
    for legibility).
  - Pictograms authored as SVG in one stroke system, not taken from an icon library.
- **First screen (phone):** the h1, then a row of figures. One figure arrives and four around it
  turn blue and link to it. The caption reads "You get in when four people vouch for you." The
  sign-up form sits below in the same screen.
- **Signature:** the status board is an Isotype legend. A solid figure is in test, a half figure is
  being built, an outline is planned, a dotted outline is research. One symbol vocabulary runs
  through the whole page.
- **Reach:** the builder page uses the same charts, and the app could take the pictograms.
- **Honest risk:** careless figures read as toilet signs or corporate clip art, so they must be
  drawn with care. The style is a century old and could read as retro.

### B. The mended net

- **Thesis:** the network is a net that members knot and mend together. Each knot joins four
  strands.
- **Own world:**
  - Light ground, net lines in brand blue, orange twine marking the newcomer's knot.
  - A rounded humanist sans.
  - The net drawn as one SVG that grows down the page.
- **First screen:** a strip of net across the top, with a new knot being tied into four strands,
  then the h1 and the form.
- **Signature:** the net extends as you scroll. Status rows read as knots tied, half-tied or loose.
- **Honest risk:** the metaphor is close to a pun ("the net"), and a net can also mean a trap.
  Thin lines are hard to draw well at 360px.

### C. Counted once (polling-station kit)

- **Thesis:** the page is a ballot slip and the ink that proves you voted once.
- **Own world:**
  - Indelible-ink violet as the committed colour, ballot-paper white with a pale security tint,
    stamp red for "planned".
  - A condensed grotesk for headings.
- **First screen:** a violet ink stripe beside the h1. The form is a ballot slip whose tick boxes
  are the three ways to take part.
- **Signature:** the status board is a ballot sheet with stamped boxes.
- **Honest risk:** the pilot holds no votes, so a ballot world promises something the product
  won't do for a long time. Election imagery can also feel risky to politically cautious readers.
  Violet competes with brand blue.

### The standing exit: the category standard, played straight

- **What it is:** a clean open-source foundation page in the manner of Signal, Matrix.org or the
  Tor Project. White ground, brand blue accent, one sans, a hero with a form, plain sections and
  a status table.
- **Honest risk:** it's familiar and effective, and it's forgettable.

## Declined

- **QSL cards (4):** strong for "Carry the network" but weak for the main promise. Kept from it:
  confirmation is person to person.
- **Hemicycle (5):** implies a parliament that doesn't exist.
- **Passport (6):** contradicts "no ID papers".
- **Literal graph (7):** too close to the rut. Kept from it: the four-link motion is shown, not
  described.
