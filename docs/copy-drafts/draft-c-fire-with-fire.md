## 2. The story

**The turn.** Technology is making our biggest problems bigger. It is not going away. So we
want to build the good version. The same kind of tools that divide people can be built to bring
them together, if the people who use them also own them.

**The hope.** In 1994, Al Gore told a meeting in Buenos Aires: "Our goal is a kind of global
conversation, in which everyone who wants can have his or her say." [check: A3]

**What happened.** Many people believe feeds tuned for anger pulled us apart. The evidence is
mixed. In one US experiment, changing people's X feeds for a single week shifted how they felt
about the other party [check: B3, Piccardi et al., *Science*, 2025]. Other large studies found
little or no effect.

**What we build.** The biggest problems do not stop at borders. A global vote only works if each
voice is one real person. That is the part we are building, and we are building it in the open.

---

## 3. What we're building

**One person, one vote** · *In test*
You get in because four people who know you vouch for you. No ID papers, no face scans, nothing
to buy. Vouching is built in the app and is being tested now.

**Your records stay yours** · *Planned*
Each person gets their own agent that keeps their records. A group sees only what belongs to
the group. Members do not run their own agents yet. That is the next step.

**A network its members carry** · *Planned*
Nodes run by people, not by one company. There is no node software to install yet. Sending
messages over radio, where the internet fails, is still research.

## 6. For builders

Each person keeps a personal ledger: their own small record, held by their agent. A contract
runs only among its members and is copied only between them. The design paper (Poupko, Shapiro,
Talmon) calls this subsidiarity: a group's data is meant to stay with the group. It is not a blockchain in
the crypto sense: no coins, no mining. The paper calls the design capital-free, so holding more
money is meant to buy no extra say. If you know Solid, it is in that family. All five properties
in the table are planned, not built. The paper also says that members of a contract who work
together could rewrite its history. We are working on that.

## 7. Where this comes from

GDI began in 2021 as a coalition of organisations working toward global democracy. That
coalition is no longer active. In 2023 we wrote a white paper, *Gloki: Global Digital Voter
Identity*. Some of its ideas have changed: today, people who know you vouch for you, not
strangers picked at random. GDI is now a project of Young World Federalists, Inc., which hosts
it. Our old motto still holds: Assume global responsibility.

## 9. Notes for Eston

**What it does well.** It answers the obvious doubt ("isn't tech the problem?") in the opening
beat, which suits world federalists who distrust platforms. The tone is warm and direct, and
uses only two sources.

**Risk.** "Technology is making our biggest problems bigger" is an opinion, and the evidence
behind it is mixed. Beat 3 says so, but a critical reader may still push back. The hero subline
puts the turn after the promise, so the promise stays word for word.

**Lines to borrow.** "The tools that divide people can bring them together, if people own them."
"A global vote only works if each voice is one real person." The board title "True today, and
still being built".
