## 2. The story

**The promise.** In 1994, Al Gore described what the new networks could become: "Our goal is a
kind of global conversation, in which everyone who wants can have his or her say." [check: A3]

**What went wrong.** We got the conversation. We did not get the say. In the US, crypto
investment fraud took $7.2 billion from people in 2025. [check: B1] Many people believe feeds
tuned for anger pulled us apart. Studies disagree. [check: B3]

**The missing part.** A vote only counts if it comes from one real person. Most online tools
check that with an email address or a phone number. Others use iris scans, passports or crypto
deposits. [check: C] Across borders, none of these works for everyone.

**What we build.** Technology is not going away, and global problems do not stop at borders. So
we are building the good version: you get in because people who know you vouch for you. No ID
papers, no face scans, no tokens. Vouching is in test. The rest is planned, and the board below
says which.

## 3. What we're building

**One person, one vote** · *In test*
You get in because four people who know you vouch for you. No ID papers, no face scans, no
tokens, nothing to buy. Vouching works in the app today and is in test.

**Your records stay yours** · *Planned*
We are building an agent for each person that keeps their own records. A group will share only
what belongs to the group. Running your own agent is planned, not done.

**A network its members carry** · *Planned*
We want nodes run by members, not by one company. You will be able to host one on a small
server. Later, where the internet fails, messages could travel over radio. That part is still
research.

## 6. For builders

In our design, each person keeps a personal ledger, held by their agent. A contract runs only
among the people who are part of it, and is copied only between them. The design paper
(Poupko, Shapiro, Talmon) calls this subsidiarity. It is capital-free. It is not a blockchain in
the crypto sense: no coins, no mining. Identity comes from a web of trust: four members who know
you vouch for you. The weak points are written down. Today your key works like a password,
signed writes are planned, and the paper notes that participants who collude can rewrite a
contract's history. If you know Solid, it is in that family.

## 7. Where this comes from

GDI began in 2021 as a coalition of organisations working toward global democracy. That
coalition is no longer active. In 2023 we wrote a white paper, *Gloki: Global Digital Voter
Identity*. Some of its ideas have changed: today, people who know you vouch for you, not
strangers picked at random. GDI is now a project of Young World Federalists, Inc., which hosts
it. Our old motto still holds: "Assume global responsibility."
