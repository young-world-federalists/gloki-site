# Thandiwe, 26, Lilongwe (Chichewa first, English third; phone, paid data bundles)

## 1. First 60 seconds

**A.** "The internet promised to bring the world together." Easy words, and I already know this hope. I read on. Under it is a long sentence about "global democracy", "no company or government is in charge", and I do not fully get it. Door: "Join the waitlist", but I want to know what I am joining.

**B.** "One person, one vote, across borders. We are building the part that is missing." What part? Then "is this one real person, counted once?" The page starts with a question about voting, not about me. I think this is for people who build software. I would probably go back to WhatsApp and ask my friend.

**C.** "There will be no global democracy without technology." I read it twice because of "no ... without". Then: "You have heard big promises about technology before. So this page shows what works today and what does not." I stay. That is how I feel about the money schemes. Door: "Join the waitlist".

## 2. Full read: where I got lost, suspicious, convinced

- **Convinced (A):** "A student in Lagos can message a student in Lima in seconds." The first time anyone like me is in the picture.
- **Convinced (C):** "Crypto promised money that no bank controls. For many people it became a way to lose money."
- **Suspicious (all):** "You get in when four people vouch for you." I know no member. Who is the first one? It also sounds like the schemes that tell you to bring four people. The page never says nobody pays or earns for this.
- **Suspicious (A/B):** "Some give more say to people who hold more digital tokens." Tokens sounds like money.
- **Suspicious (all):** the node is called "Identity Block Chain". The page just told me crypto is a problem, then uses the word.
- **Lost (B):** the second half. "Semaphore", "ElectionGuard threshold keys", "Tor onion services", "Reticulum over LoRa", "USSD", "CSP/SRI". Many brackets explain the brackets. I stopped reading.
- **Lost (all):** the 11-row status table. On a small screen, with data costing money, I would not scroll it.
- **Honesty:** "Until encryption is built, the project team can see your data." I trust them more for saying it, but I ask who the team is.
- **Missing everywhere:** climate, jobs, Africa. My friend said "young Africans for a pilot". No page says where the pilot is, who it is for or when. I would doubt the link was real.

**Words and sentences a third-language reader trips on**
- "A Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace", "Davos", "civilization of the Mind" (A, B, C): old Western reference, hard words, no meaning for me.
- "keeps us scrolling" (A), "Crypto burned a lot of trust", "walking away from technology", "hype" (C): idioms and slang.
- "There will be no global democracy without technology" (A section title, B hero, C hero): double negative.
- "a city or national register", "the organiser's list" (A, B, C): "register" and the British spelling are unfamiliar.
- "binding vote" (B, C): only A explains it.
- "Carry the network" (all): a metaphor with no clue what I would do. "Get in" and "vouch" are also unclear.
- "fiscal host", "coalition", "white paper", "pilot", "waitlist", "Sybil attack", "consensus", "GPL-3.0", "Docker image": I know none of them.
- The promise sentence, 30 words with three clauses (all). Too long for a phone.

## 4. Findings

1. **Blocker.** All drafts, "Join Gloki" door and waitlist form: nothing says it is free, what the pilot is, or what happens after I give my email. Add: "Joining the waitlist is free. We will never ask you for money." Say what the email will bring (A/B/C only say "Check your inbox"), and what the pilot asks of me.
2. **Major.** "You get in when four people vouch for you" (all): answer "I know no one in it" and "does anyone get paid for vouching?". The first members need a clear answer.
3. **Major.** "Identity Block Chain" and "tokens" (A, B, C): next to the crypto-fraud paragraph, it reads as a scheme. Keep the name only in the builders' door; tell me the network is not a coin.
4. **Major.** No African or youth view anywhere. Add one line on where members come from, and why it matters for climate or jobs. If the pilot is not aimed at young Africans, say so, so my friend and I are not misled.
5. **Major.** Reading level: cut Barlow, "Davos", "Cyberspace", the idioms listed above, and the "no ... without" opening. Break the promise sentence into two or three short ones.
6. **Major.** Length and data cost (B 2800 words, A 2400, C 2200). Put the Join door and one-paragraph "what you get" near the top. Collapse the status table; for me a plain list of "works now / being built" is enough.
7. **Minor.** "no company or government is in charge" (all) can sound like nobody is responsible. Say who to ask if something goes wrong.
8. **Minor.** Buttondown and YWF: say in one plain sentence who can see my email and what "your data" means.
