# Review by Kofi (30, policy officer, Accra; Young World Federalists member)

## 1. First 60 seconds

**A.** "The internet promised to bring the world together." I nod: that was my generation's first hope. "Open network for global democracy... each person counts once" tells me this is about voting. I keep reading and would click "Join the waitlist".

**B.** "One person, one vote, across borders" is the most federalist line in any draft, and I like it. "We are building the part that is missing" and two buttons make it sound like a startup pitch. I keep reading, a little less warm.

**C.** "There will be no global democracy without technology." Bold, and I half agree. Then "You have heard big promises about technology before" puts my guard up. I keep reading, wary.

## 2. Full read

- **Where is the democracy?** All three drafts are about who counts (identity). None says what people would decide, or how this relates to a world parliament. "No company or government is in charge" (all drafts) sounds anarchist to a federalist: I want democratic institutions, not their absence.
- **Barlow quote** (all drafts). His declaration told governments they had no sovereignty in cyberspace. A federalist will know that, so quoting him for a democracy project is odd.
- **A** works best. "A student in Lagos can message a student in Lima in seconds" speaks to Accra, and "It did not give us a fair way to decide anything together" is the best sentence on the topic.
- **B** loses me in "How it works, for builders": "Semaphore", "ElectionGuard threshold keys", "CSP/SRI", "Reticulum over LoRa". It makes me feel I'm not the audience.
- **C** lists too many grievances. "Crypto burned a lot of trust. We feel it every time we tell someone we are building a network" makes me link "network" to crypto, and "Identity Block Chain" confirms it.
- **Status table** (all). I respect the honesty, but it is mostly "Being built / Planned / Research" and reads as "very little exists".
- **Pilot door.** A says it plainly: "one shared forum... Ways to discuss and vote together come later." C says only "we will write to you when there is a place." I don't know what I would do, who else is there, or what we would discuss. Honest, but a forum with nothing to vote on feels like a WhatsApp group with extra steps. It reassures me about honesty and deflates me about purpose.
- **History.** The honesty is good: "Where the paper and the app differ, the app tells you what is true now" (A). But "The coalition is no longer active" comes with no reason, and I remember 2021. Who were the seven organisations (B and C say seven; A does not)? "GDI is now a project of Young World Federalists, Inc." is cold: am I, as a member, already part of this? B at least gives the paper's title.
- **Momentum.** No dates, no next milestone, no people beyond Ouri and Eston. "Coalition no longer active", then a table of unfinished parts: not stuck, but not visibly going anywhere.

## 3. Ranking

1. **A.** Shortest path from hope to door; best pilot explanation; warmest voice.
2. **C.** Strongest opening line and clear doors, but defensive and grievance-heavy.
3. **B.** Best headline, but the longest (about 2,800 words) and the most technical.

## 4. Findings

1. **Major** (all): "no company or government is in charge". A federalist asks how this fits with democratic world institutions. Fix: one sentence placing GDI beside the federalist project and saying what the network is for.
2. **Major** (C "Join Gloki"): "Join the waitlist for the pilot, and we will write to you when there is a place." Fix: "You will post and reply with other members in one shared forum. There is nothing to vote on yet."
3. **Major** (B "How it works, for builders"): jargon wall. Fix: link out, keep one builder paragraph.
4. **Major** (A, B, C history): "The coalition is no longer active." No reason, no names, and the YWF link says nothing about membership. Fix: one honest line on what happened, name the seven if possible, say what YWF membership means here.
5. **Major** (all, Barlow quote): his libertarian stance clashes with the project. Fix: pair it with "the hope was right; the institutions never came", or drop it.
6. **Minor** (C): "Crypto burned a lot of trust. We feel it every time we tell someone we are building a network." Fix: cut crypto or keep only the FBI figure.
7. **Minor** (B): "the project team can see your data" appears three times. Fix: say it once, near the form.

## 6. Would I act?

**Yes.** I would join the Gloki waitlist and forward the page to the YWF group, adding "read the status table". The 1990s story is moving only in A, in two short sections with the Lagos/Lima line. In C it becomes a list of tech complaints and feels beside the point. What would firm up my yes: a line saying what the network is *for* in democratic terms, and what my first week in the forum looks like. What would stop me forwarding to academics: no answer on how "no government in charge" fits with world federalism.
