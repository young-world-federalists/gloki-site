We got the connection. We did not get the say.

“The Web is more a social creation than a technical one. I designed it for a social effect—to help people work together—and not as a technical toy.”

Tim Berners-Lee, who invented the Web, in Weaving the Web, 1999

In the US, people reported losing $7.2 billion to crypto investment fraud in 2025. Many people believe feeds tuned for anger pulled us apart. The evidence is mixed.

A vote only counts if it comes from one real person. Online, one person can make a hundred accounts.

Some online tools check that with an email address or a phone number. Others use iris scans, passports or crypto deposits. Across borders, none of these works for everyone.

Technology is not going away, and global problems do not stop at borders. So we are building the good version, starting from people.

What we're building

Climate, pandemics and war do not stop at borders. People who work for a democratic world have proposed answers: a world parliament, global citizens' assemblies, worldwide votes on a question. Each one needs something first: a way to know that each voice belongs to one real person, counted once, in any country.

  • One person, one vote

    In test

    You get in because four people who know you vouch for you. To vouch means to confirm that you are a real person. No ID papers, no face scans, no tokens, nothing to buy.

  • Your own record

    Planned

    We are building a record for each person that they control, not a company. A group will share only what belongs to the group.

  • A network its members carry

    Planned

    We want nodes run by members, not by one company. A node is a small server that keeps a copy of the network. Later, where the internet fails, messages could travel over radio. That part is still research.

Live

  • A demo of the democracy app. Seeded data, runs in your browser. Open the demo.
  • A node developers can run on their own computer. For building and testing apps. It is not safe to run in public yet. How the node works.

In test

  • Sign-up with a key, three kinds of account, one shared forum. Not yet: today the key works like a password: whoever types it in is you.
  • Getting verified by people who know you. In the test version you ask someone who is already verified. The aim is four people who know you; the network does not enforce that yet.
  • Joining a community. Works in the test version, in a technical way for now.

Planned

  • Your own record, which you control. For now, records are kept on the project's servers.
  • Nodes hosted by members. The node is not safe to run in public yet.
  • Nobody can act as you. Every action will be signed with a key only you hold. Not yet: today the network checks no signatures. This needs more builders. The known gaps.
  • Nobody can quietly rewrite the record. Copies will be kept by many members, so a change gets caught.
  • Nobody can read your ballot. Voting is not part of the beta.
  • You can leave and take your identity with you.
  • Losing your phone doesn't lose your vote. The people who vouched for you will be able to help you back in.

Research

  • Messages over radio where there is no internet. We are still finding out if it can work.

A rule we keep today

There will be no binding vote on this network before an independent security audit. A binding vote is any vote whose result we present as a decision of the members. We will publish the full audit report, including any problems not yet fixed.

The beta

The beta is the first group of people on Gloki. It is free: nothing to pay, no tokens, nothing to buy.

  • Who it is for: world citizens, starting with the Young World Federalists' chapters and others who already work across borders. Anyone can join the waitlist.
  • What you do: make a key, get vouched for by four people who know you, and post in one shared forum. Discussing and voting together come later.
  • Who vouches first: the first few members vouch for each other, starting with the two of us.
  • When it opens: once your phone can sign what you do, so nobody else can act as you. We will write to everyone on the waitlist then.

Before you join. Ouri Poupko runs the beta's node on Google Cloud. Until encryption is built, the project team can see your data.
