There will be no global democracy without technology.

The Global Democracy Initiative is building an open network for global democracy, where every member is a real person, each person counts once, and no company or government is in charge.

You have heard big promises about technology before. So this page shows what works today and what does not.

The internet connected us

In the 1990s, many people hoped the internet would bring the world together. Anyone could talk to anyone, across any border.

Part of that came true. A student in Lagos can message a student in Lima in seconds.

But the internet never learned who is real. One person can make a hundred accounts. A computer program can pretend to be a person. [figure on fake or automated accounts: source needed] When you cannot tell real people from fake ones, you cannot count opinions fairly, and you cannot hold a fair vote.

In many countries, more people now say the internet and social media have made people more divided. [Pew Research Center 2026: check wording and figure] Online scams have grown too. In 2025, people in the United States reported losing [FBI IC3 2025: crypto losses, about US$11.4 billion, verify] to scams that used cryptocurrency.

The internet connected us. It did not give us a fair way to decide anything together.

What the network is for

Some problems belong to everyone. Climate, pandemics and war do not stop at borders. Yet there is no place where people from every country can raise a shared problem, talk it through and decide together.

People who work for a democratic world have proposed answers: a world parliament, global citizens' assemblies, worldwide votes on a question. Each one needs something first: a way to know that each voice belongs to one real person, counted once, in any country.

The whole world will never meet in one hall. If people everywhere are going to decide things together, a network has to carry their voices. That is why we still build with technology.

One person, one vote, across borders

Today's democracy tools decide who counts with a government's list of residents, a list from the organiser, tokens, or no check at all. Each fits the job it was made for, but none lets people everywhere take part, one vote each, without a government, a company or money in the middle. That is the part we are building. How other tools decide who counts.

You get in when four people vouch for you. To vouch means to confirm that you are a real person. You do not upload an ID card, scan your face or pay anything.

We have not yet chosen the rule for which four people vouch for you. That rule decides how well the network keeps out fake accounts, so we make no promise about fake accounts until it is chosen. When it is, this page will say which rule we picked and why.

What works today, and what does not

Last updated: 26 October 2026.

  • Works now
  • Being built
  • Planned or possible
  • Research

In the demo app

  • Four people vouch for you. The app shows the steps; the network does not run the rule yet.
  • One shared forum, to post and reply.

For developers

  • A node you can run on your own computer. A node is a small server that keeps a copy of the network. Not safe to run in public yet.

Early code

  • Copying records to many nodes. Not yet checked or tested across separate computers.

Being built

  • Nobody can act as you. Not yet: today the network does not check who sends a request, and other members can see your key.
  • Servers that keep your data without being able to read it. Not yet: until then, the project team can see your data.

Possible, not used yet

  • Your data on your own node. In the demo, everyone's data sits on one node.

Planned

  • Secret votes. Nobody can see how you voted.
  • Leave with your identity.
  • Lose your phone, keep your vote.

Research

  • Reaching places without internet, by radio. We are still finding out if it can work.

A rule we keep today

There will be no binding vote on this network before an independent security audit. A binding vote is any vote whose result we present as a decision of the members. We will publish the full audit report, including any problems not yet fixed.

The pilot

The pilot is the first group of people on Gloki. It is free: nothing to pay, no tokens, nothing to buy.

  • Who it is for: world citizens, starting with the Young World Federalists' chapters and others who already work across borders. Anyone can sign up.
  • What you do: make a key, get vouched for by four people, and post in one shared forum. Discussing and voting together come later.
  • Who vouches first: the first few members vouch for each other, starting with the two of us.
  • When it opens: once your phone can sign what you do, so nobody else can act as you. We will write to everyone who signed up then.

Before you join. Ouri Poupko runs the pilot's node on Google Cloud. Until encryption is built, the project team can see your data. Later your account may need to move to the full network; we plan a tool that moves it for you, so nobody is vouched for twice.
