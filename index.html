There will be no global democracy without technology.

We are building the part every democracy app is missing: one real person, one vote, on a network its members carry.

Gloki is the foundation we are building: one identity per person, vouched for by people who know you, with your own record that you control.

Why we are building it

“The Web is more a social creation than a technical one. I designed it for a social effect—to help people work together—and not as a technical toy.”

Tim Berners-Lee, who invented the Web, in Weaving the Web, 1999

We got the connection. We did not get the say.

In the US, people reported losing $7.2 billion to crypto investment fraud in 2025. Many people believe feeds tuned for anger pulled us apart. The evidence is mixed.

A vote only counts if it comes from one real person. Some online tools check that with an email address or a phone number. Others use iris scans, passports or crypto deposits. Across borders, none of these works for everyone.

Technology is not going away, and global problems do not stop at borders. So we are building the good version, starting from people.

What we're building

  1. You In test

    You sign up with a key, not a password. No ID papers, no face scans, nothing to buy.

  2. Four people vouch In test

    Four people who know you confirm you are a real person. That is how we get to one person, one vote.

  3. Your own record Planned

    We are building a record for each person that they control, not a company. A group will share only what belongs to the group.

  4. Members carry the network Planned

    We want nodes run by members, not by one company. Later, where the internet fails, messages could travel over radio. That part is still research.

For builders

In our design, each person keeps a personal ledger: their own record. A contract runs only among the people who are part of it, and is copied only between them. The design paper (Poupko, Shapiro, Talmon) calls this subsidiarity. It is capital-free. It is not a blockchain in the crypto sense: no coins, no mining. Identity comes from a web of trust: four members who know you vouch for you.

The weak points are written down. Today your key works like a password, signed writes are planned, and the paper notes that participants who collude can rewrite a contract's history. If you know Solid, it is in that family. The source is on GitHub.

Where this comes from

Assume global responsibility

GDI began in 2021 as a coalition of organisations working toward global democracy. That coalition is no longer active. In 2024 we published a white paper, Gloki: Global Digital Voter Identity. Some of its ideas have changed: today, people who know you vouch for you, not strangers picked at random.
